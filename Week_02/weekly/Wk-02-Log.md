# Week 02 — Foundations and intrusion detection

The foundations exercises connected the linear algebra in my learning plan to a working PyTorch model. I followed the shapes from a batch of inputs through a manual linear layer, ReLU and the output layer. Comparing manual gradients against autograd gave me a concrete way to check a transpose or a bias reduction. The softmax exercise also showed why subtracting the largest logit matters when inputs have very different magnitudes. On MNIST, the MLP reached 88.42% test accuracy after one epoch on 10,000 training images. That run checked that the data loader, loss, optimizer and evaluation code worked together on real data.

For the intrusion experiment, I compared the majority class, logistic regression and a two-layer MLP on UNSW-NB15 network flows. I kept the published test partition separate, removed exact duplicate feature vectors, and fitted the encoder and scaler on the training split. With a fixed split, I could vary the training seed and learning rate while comparing the models on the same flows. Recording validation loss by epoch made it possible to see when another training epoch stopped helping. The best learning rate also varied across seeds.

The most surprising result was how much the conclusion changed with the alert budget. At the default threshold, the seed-42 model found 3,418 attacks and missed only 188. It also flagged 1,960 benign flows. When I limited the false-positive rate to 1%, the same model detected about 56% of attacks. That comparison made the cost of a false alarm much easier to explain than a single accuracy number. The ROC and precision–recall plots also made it easier to compare the models across thresholds.

My next step would be to choose an alert threshold on validation data and measure how that fixed threshold behaves on a later capture. I would also break missed detections down by attack category. The current results give me a reproducible benchmark for those questions, while the three seeds only show training variability on one split.
